# 02 / Halfmile
**Study classification:** narrative · poster hierarchy · time-based information.

**Historical source:** `halfmile-landing.html` (not republished). Standalone HTML/CSS/JS with Canvas 2D; source type families include Anton, Archivo and JetBrains Mono. The route and minute values in the concept are illustration, **not actual live tracking**.

## What the source demonstrates
**Poster structure.** A repeatable modular `.poster-grid` and asymmetric supporting content make the page feel connected to dispatch posters and city wayfinding. The graphic grammar comes from a physical cultural reference, not a generic component library.

**Progress choreography.** Distinct outer scroll stages and inner sticky scenes translate scroll position into a sequence. A “copy wipe” uses duplicate text layers plus clipping. Other sections share an abstract progress parameter that drives a timeline, active phase, simulated clock and deterministic route diagram.

**Fallback design.** A reduced-motion mode transforms lengthy pinned sections into normal document flow rather than merely disabling transitions on a still-overlong page.

## Transferable mechanism
One verified process model can drive multiple views: time, sequence, diagram and written explanation. A spatial representation should help people comprehend a real journey; otherwise a plain schedule wins.

## What not to carry over
Red/cream/black palette, Anton wordmark, faux logistics metrics, kilometer lines masquerading as real GPS data, or many 250–400vh pinned stages. Staggered motion is not an alternative to a clear document.

## A better reuse
A performing artist could reveal an actual show's cue sequence; a chef could illustrate an authentic recipe timeline. A courier aesthetic makes little sense for either. Structure and typography should derive from their real material.

## QA
Short browser heights, reversing scroll direction, keyboard section access, touch, accurate numbers, no blocked reading, and a truly linear reduced-motion presentation.
